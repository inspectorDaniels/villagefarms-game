## Verdict
The astronomy is the best thing in this module. I checked shadow direction and length against 51°N and they are right: WSW at 05:00 in June, due W at 07:00, NNE and short at 13:00, ESE and capped at 8× at 19:30, and due N and long at a January noon (june_0500, noon_0700, june_1300, golden_1930, jan_1200). Overcast correctly removes the shadows. The problem is the light the sun produces. Nights are close to black and turn unreadable in the game as soon as it rains. Golden hour turns the grass khaki instead of making it glow. Fog and snow show streaky, stretched-noise columns. Rain is the stock pattern of evenly spaced white hairlines. The cloud shadows, which should be the headline feature, can hardly be seen on a cloudy day. This is a correct simulation behind a grade that is not yet painted-indie quality. It is not shippable yet.

## Must fix (ordered)
1. **Nights are unreadable, and rainy nights are pure black.** game_night.png (March, 23:00, rain) averages RGB [15,18,24] over the play area: terrain, river and field edges are all gone. Even the clear full-moon showcase night averages [25,34,39] (preset_night.png) and the paths barely read. The cause is that the ambient floor `[32,40,76]` in `sky.js ambientFor` sits *below* the brief's ≈[38,48,92], and then `fx.drawScreen` stacks more darkening on top of the multiply: a soft-light blue wash at 0.55·night, 0.28·night desaturation, and a vignette of up to 0.62 (0.2+0.28 night+0.08 rain). Fix it like this: the floor must hold *after* the grade, so move the floor up to about the brief's [38,48,92] for the new-moon/rain worst case. Cap the night vignette at about 0.35. Stop the night soft-light wash from darkening (use a lighten-safe tint, or apply it only to the ambient colour). Aim for a rainy new-moon night where fields, paths and the river silhouette stay legible at a glance (mean luminance of roughly 35–45 on grass).
2. **The golden-hour grade is muddy, not golden.** preset_golden.png and golden_1930.png: the whole field goes olive/khaki and the reds go brown. The amber ambient multiply plus the orange `soft-light` gradient on green gives mud. Golden hour needs warm, *saturated* highlights on the sunlit side and relatively cooler shadows. Reduce the multiply amber at 3–12° elevation (for example keep G ≥ 0.85·R), replace the soft-light orange with a lower-alpha `overlay`/`screen` warm tint that lifts rather than dirties, and do not apply the overcast desaturation when `golden > 0`.
3. **Fog (and the fog component of snow) reads as a stretched-noise overlay.** preset_fog.png, fog_0700.png, snow_1230.png: strong vertical column streaks run across the whole screen. They come from the anisotropic pattern stretch (`tile(... ang, 2.2 / 1.6)`) aligned with the wind, which here is roughly N–S. On top of that, the veil plus two bank layers wash out about 70% of the contrast (the fence and wall almost vanish in fog_0700). Drop the stretch to ≤1.3 or rotate the two layers by different angles so they don't reinforce each other. Soften the bank edges (the `smooth(lo,hi)` window is too narrow at 160 px per 150 m). Cap the combined fog opacity so gameplay objects keep about 50% contrast. Bank placement should also vary with some low-frequency depth, not tile uniformly.
4. **The `closeup` preset is not "at dusk" as the README claims.** preset_closeup.png at 21:50 in July is full night (mean [42,49,45]), and the lamp pools read sickly yellow-green. Either move it to about 20:45 so the lamp shows against blue-hour light, or rename it. A showcase preset has to demonstrate what it claims.

## Should fix
- **Cloud shadows are nearly invisible** (preset_default.png at April 10:30, cloudy 0.6): with `shadowStrength = 0.45·(1−0.85·0.48) ≈ 0.27`, the drifting blobs are indistinguishable from the showcase's ground mottling. They only read over the empty void in cloudy_far.png. I understand the brief formula forces this and that core request #1 is filed. Until it lands, at least make the cloud texture read (a harder core with a softer rim, and fewer, bigger clouds) so there's a clear sun/shade patchwork. Re-evaluate once core supports `cloudShadowStrength`.
- **Rain is a generic hairline overlay** (rain_1230.png, rain_close.png, storm_1930.png): streaks are uniformly spaced, identical in angle, and the same weight at every depth. From a top-down camera, add short near-drops with visible length variation and density clumping (gust sheets driven by `windAt`), and let the far layer be a soft haze of shorter ticks. Splashes belong to effects, but expose the drop rate so effects can sync.
- **Lightning is a flat whitewash** (storm_flash.png): the whole screen lifts to milky pale green. A strike should give a very brief, *hard*, cool light with a sudden shadow direction (for example set `sun.dir` toward `flashAt` with shadowStrength ~0.3 for the pulse), then drop back to darker than before.
- **Lightning timing is frame-rate dependent.** `flash.t += dt` and `flashRng` are consumed in `frame()`, so the strike schedule (and `env:lightning` for audio) differs between 30 and 144 fps and between runs. Move the schedule into `update()` (fixed step) and keep only the pulse envelope in `frame()`.
- **Twilight darkens too fast.** At 21:00 in June (sun ≈ −6°, civil twilight) the scene is already nearly as dark as midnight (june_2100.png [42,53,50] vs night [25,34,39]), because `night = 1 − daylight` and daylight is `smooth(−9,5)`. The blue hour should be a visibly distinct, fairly bright, blue-violet state. Also consider documenting or offsetting the "summer sunset 20:15" (the clock is mean solar time). Belgian players expect light until about 22:00.
- `setWeather(kind, 0)` can't set intensity 0 (`clamp(...) || 0.7`).
- Dawn/dusk events can fire spuriously on clock jumps (`resync` doesn't reset `prevSunUp`).
- `load()` values for wetness and snowCover are discarded if the load also moves the clock by more than 1 h (`update` → `resync()` zeroes them). Either skip the resync right after a load or document that it happens.
- Puddles in the showcase are drawn as hard, dark, outlined blobs at night (preset_night.png) and blank white disks in the storm (storm_1930.png). They are showcase-only, but they're the reference for how `sky` and `wetness` should be used, so make them look right.
- Cross-module note (not environment's bug): game_rain.png shows a rectangular seam where some terrain chunks repainted "wet" and others didn't. Terrain should quantise `weather.wetness`. Environment could help by publishing a stepped `wetnessLevel` (e.g. 0.05 steps).

## What works
- Solar geometry is correct and well implemented: NOAA declination and equation of time, refraction, the shadow unit vector, and `shadowLen = min(8, 1/tan)`. Every probe I ran matched the expected azimuth and length for 51°N.
- Shadow fade at sunset and with cloud, fog and precipitation behaves correctly. Overcast gives no shadows (overcast_noon.png), and moon shadows on clear full-moon nights are a nice touch.
- Weather is deterministic and seasonal: a pressure-noise regime, 6 h segments, climate rules for convective storms, radiation fog and snow, a sensible forecast, and wetness/snow integration with a 12 h replay on clock jumps.
- The API and contract are clean, `windAt` is advected gust noise (good for foliage), and in-game cost is 0.25–0.7 ms.
- Snow flake sheets (snow_close.png) are soft and parallax-layered. They read as snowfall, not as dots.
- The showcase is thoughtful: poplars for long shadows, bare winter oaks with lace shadows (jan_1200.png), a lamp and door light, and a windsock following `windAt`.
